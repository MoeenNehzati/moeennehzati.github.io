  <p>I'm a Ph.D. student in the NYU Department of Economics. My interests are primarily methodological: I like playing with mathematical and computational ideas, and right now I'm applying them to problems in economics.</p>
  <p>My current work draws on geometric and measure-theoretic ideas in high- and infinite-dimensional spaces. I study genericity and prevalence, optimal transport, and nonconvex optimization, mostly with applications to economic and statistical models.</p>
  <p>I'm bad at bookkeeping, but fairly crafty about getting computers to compensate for it. Over the years, that has meant simulation, symbolic computation, and all sorts of computational sanity checks. These days, the new toy is AI. I'm particularly interested in AI for science and agentic programming, and <a href="https://github.com/MoeenNehzati/famulus" target="_blank">Famulus</a> is my main experiment in getting more leverage out of these tools for research and programming.</p>
  <p>More broadly, my theoretical interests range across analysis, algorithms and complexity, mathematical logic, optimization, and related areas of mathematics and theoretical computer science. Before NYU, I worked on large-scale statistical genetics at UCLA and as an auction design consultant at Cafe Bazaar, optimizing online ad auctions. A while before all that, I earned my B.Sc. in Computer Engineering from Sharif University of Technology.</p>
  <p>Outside of work, I get overly excited by cats, cooking and baking, fantasy (unfortunately not LOTR), modern history—especially the Middle East—foundations of mathematics, and getting out into nature for backpacking.</p>
